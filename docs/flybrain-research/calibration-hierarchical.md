# Research notes: calibration-hierarchical (F6, plus F1/F5/F9/F10)

Date: 2026-09-24. Read-only literature pass. Every source below was fetched (the abstract page at minimum). Quotes come from the fetched page.

## Problem restated
cell_class ECE is 0.17-0.43 while coarse super_class ECE is 0.005-0.04. The learners are LR, HistGradientBoosting and multinomial NB. Splits are grouped by cell_type, hemilineage and L/R pair, so the test set is shifted relative to the training set.

## Sources

1. Guo et al. 2017, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017, arXiv:1706.04599. The paper says "temperature scaling -- a single-parameter variant of Platt Scaling -- is surprisingly effective". METHOD for F6. It is the baseline recalibrator: it fits one parameter and cannot change the ranking. Caveats: the paper studies neural networks on i.i.d. splits.
2. Kull et al. 2019, "Beyond temperature scaling: ... Dirichlet calibration", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1910.12656. The method is "natively multiclass ... applicable to classifiers from any model class" and is "equivalent to log-transforming the uncalibrated probabilities, followed by one linear layer and softmax". It improves confidence-ECE, classwise-ECE, log-loss and Brier. METHOD for F6. It works for HGB, LR and NB. Use off-diagonal regularization (ODIR) when there are many classes.
3. Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005, "Predicting Good Probabilities With Supervised Learning", ICML 2005 (PDF fetched). Findings: "boosted trees ... push probability mass away from 0 and 1"; "Naive Bayes ... push probabilities toward 0 and 1"; "Isotonic Regression is more prone to overfitting ... when data is scarce"; "after calibration the best methods are boosted trees, random forests and SVMs". CONFIRMS/EXTENDS F5 and F6. The NB failures are expected, and HGB needs post-hoc calibration. Isotonic regression is risky for rare cell_classes.
4. Zadrozny & Elkan 2002, "Transforming Classifier Scores into Accurate Multiclass Probability Estimates", KDD 2002, DOI 10.1145/775047.775151 (PDF fetched). The paper says "Previous calibration methods apply only to two-class problems"; its approach decomposes into binary problems, calibrates each and combines them. The NB scores it studies are "typically too extreme". METHOD (one-vs-rest isotonic regression or PAV, then renormalize) for F6. CONFIRMS F5 on NB.
5. Nixon et al. 2019, "Measuring Calibration in Deep Learning", CVPRW 2019, arXiv:1904.01685. The paper says "expected calibration error, the most popular metric, has numerous flaws". The "rank ordering of recalibration methods is drastically impacted by the choice of calibration measure". It recommends class-conditioning, L2 and adaptive bins (SCE/ACE). METHOD for F6. Report classwise or adaptive ECE for cell_class as well as top-label ECE.
6. Kumar, Liang & Ma 2019, "Verified Uncertainty Calibration", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1909.10155. The paper says "Platt scaling and temperature scaling are (i) less calibrated than reported". The scaling-binning calibrator needs O(1/eps^2 + B) samples, and the debiased estimator needs O(sqrt(B)). METHOD for F6. The paper warns that plug-in ECE is biased.
7. Roelofs et al. 2022, "Mitigating Bias in Calibration Error Estimation", AISTATS 2022, arXiv:2012.08668. It finds that "bins of equal mass ... have lower bias than ... equal width" and proposes ECE_sweep. METHOD for F6. Part of the 0.17-0.43 may be estimator bias on small per-class test sets. Recompute with equal-mass bins or ECE_sweep, and bootstrap the result.
8. Gupta & Ramdas 2022, "Top-label calibration and multiclass-to-binary reductions", ICLR 2022, arXiv:2107.08353. The approach calibrates "conditioned on the top-label" through M2B reductions with histogram binning, which is "distribution-free". METHOD for F6. Class-conditional top-label calibration fits a many-class HGB.
9. LeCoz, Herbin & Adjed 2024, "Confidence Calibration of Classifiers with Many Classes", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2411.02988. The paper says "many confidence calibration methods fail for problems with many classes". Its Top-versus-All reduction turns calibration into one binary problem. METHOD for F6 and a direct match for cell_class. Caveat: it was evaluated on neural networks only.
10. Resin, Yang & Gneiting 2026, "Hierarchies of Calibration: Classification meets Regression", arXiv:2606.03245 (fetched via alphaXiv). The paper says "Auto-calibration implies class-wise calibration, which implies marginal calibration". It also defines confidence calibration and modal calibration. EXTENDS F6: we must state which calibration notion our ECE measures. Coarse-level calibration is a weaker property and does not imply fine-level calibration.
11. Manokhin & Grønhaug 2026, "Classifier Calibration at Scale", arXiv:2601.19944. The paper says "Platt scaling and isotonic regression, can systematically degrade proper scoring performance for strong modern tabular models"; Venn-Abers and Beta calibration performed best; "No method dominates uniformly". CONTRADICTS the naive plan of applying isotonic regression everywhere. It is directly relevant to HGB. Caveat: binary tasks only.
12. Ovadia et al. 2019, "Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty?", NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.02530. The paper says "Traditional post-hoc calibration does indeed fall short" under shift; "methods which marginalize over models give surprisingly strong results". EXTENDS F6 and F9. Our grouped split is a form of shift, so fit calibrators on a grouped calibration fold, and try ensembles.
13. Redmon & Farhadi 2017, "YOLO9000" (WordTree), CVPR 2017, arXiv:1612.08242 (PDF fetched). The method predicts "conditional probabilities at every node" and multiplies them along the path. It uses "softmax over all synsets that are hyponyms of the same concept". Accuracy is "71.9% top-1" versus a flat baseline, a marginal drop. It falls back gracefully: it "will still predict 'dog' with high confidence". METHOD for F6 and F10. The factorization is P(type)=P(type|class)P(class|super_class)P(super_class). This gives hierarchy-consistent probabilities, and decisions can stop at the deepest confident level.
14. Bertinetto et al. 2020, "Making Better Mistakes", CVPR 2020, arXiv:1912.09393. The paper says mistakes are "equally likely to be absurd" under flat cross-entropy. It proposes hierarchical cross-entropy and soft labels, and evaluates mistake severity via hierarchical distance. METHOD for F6. Report the LCA-depth or mistake severity of cell_class errors.
15. Kosmopoulos et al. 2013, "Evaluation Measures for Hierarchical Classification: a unified view and novel approaches", arXiv:1306.6802. It analyses set-based measures (hP, hR, hF, following Kiritchenko 2006) and pair-based ones. METHOD: hierarchical F1 for super_class > class > type.
16. Silla & Freitas 2011, "A survey of hierarchical classification across different application domains", DMKD 22:31-72, DOI 10.1007/s10618-010-0175-9 (landing page seen via search only; the full text was not fetched). It gives the taxonomy of approaches: flat, local per node, local per parent node, and global. It is relevant to the F10 router design. verified=false (search snippet only).
17. Michielsen, Reinders & Mahfouz 2021, "Hierarchical progressive learning of cell identities in single-cell data" (scHPL), Nat Commun, DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-23196-8 (PMC8121839 fetched). It uses a hierarchical classifier tree with rejection via PCA reconstruction error (99th percentile) and a linear or one-class SVM. The flat SVMrejection F1 is 0.98 "but with 19.8% rejected cells"; the hierarchical linear SVM median F1 is 0.94. METHOD for F6 and F10: rejection or stopping at an internal node. EXTENDS F9 through cross-dataset annotations at different resolutions.
18. Ergen et al. 2024, "Consensus prediction of cell type labels ... popV", Nat Genet, bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.08.18.553912. It is "an ensemble of prediction models with an ontology-based voting scheme" that "provides effective uncertainty scores". METHOD: ontology-aware ensemble agreement as a confidence measure. Its numbers were not extractable from the abstract.
19. Schlegel et al. 2024, "Whole-brain annotation and multi-connectome cell typing of Drosophila", Nature 634:139, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5 (PMC11446831 fetched). The paper says "flow > superclass > class > cell type". The labels rest on anatomy, development and coarse morphology, and connectivity was used for across-dataset typing. 56% of hemibrain types matched unambiguously and 32% were not re-identified. CONFIRMS F8's circularity concern at the type level. It supplies the hierarchy needed for consistent probabilities. Label noise at fine levels caps the calibration we can reach.
20. Ding et al. 2023, "Class-Conditional Conformal Prediction with Many Classes", NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.09335. The paper says "existing conformal prediction methods do not work well when there is a limited amount of labeled data per class"; clustered conformal is its fix. METHOD for F6 with rare cell_classes.
21. Mortier et al. 2025/2026, "Conformal Prediction in Hierarchical Classification with Constrained Representation Complexity", arXiv:2501.19038. It "returns internal nodes as prediction sets" with nominal coverage. The related work den Hengst et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.13288 (HCC), builds prediction sets from nodes at mixed levels. METHOD for F6 and F10.

## Synthesis
- The high cell_class ECE is expected, not anomalous. HGB shows sigmoid-type underconfidence and overconfidence (3). NB is extreme (3, 4). Top-label ECE is biased upward when per-class test counts are small (6, 7). Grouped splits are shift, and calibrators fit on i.i.d. data fail under shift (12).
- Before fixing anything, re-measure: use equal-mass or ECE_sweep, classwise ECE and a debiased estimator with bootstrap CIs, reported per model, and add log-loss and Brier. Name the calibration notion used (10).
- Recalibrate on a grouped held-out calibration fold. Candidates are temperature scaling (LR), Dirichlet with ODIR (2), Top-versus-All (9) and Venn-Abers or beta (11). Avoid per-class isotonic regression for rare classes (3, 11).
- Hierarchy: factor probabilities along super_class > class > type (13) and enforce consistency. Report hierarchical F1 (15) and mistake severity (14). Allow abstention at an internal node (17, 21). Pair this with clustered or hierarchical conformal sets for coverage guarantees (20, 21). This is also a concrete, validated form of the F10 router: a local-per-parent-node classifier (16).
- Label-noise caveat: fine labels are partly connectivity-derived and have reconciliation errors (19). Fine-level probabilities cannot be calibrated against noisy targets beyond the noise floor.
