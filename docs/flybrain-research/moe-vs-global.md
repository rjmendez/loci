# moe-vs-global: research notes (2026-09-24)
Topic: region-specialized experts + router vs single global model (F10, F5). Read-only research; no code changes.

## Sources (fetched unless marked)
1. Montero-Manso & Hyndman, "Principles and algorithms for forecasting groups of time series: Locality and globality", IJF 2021, arXiv:2008.00444. "Global methods are not more restrictive than local methods"; "complexity of local methods grows with the size of the set while it remains constant for global methods"; global linear models competitive "with two orders of magnitude fewer parameters". -> CONTRADICTS premise of F10; SUPPORTS F1/F5.
2. Kratzert et al., "Towards learning universal, regional, and local hydrological behaviors via ML applied to large-sample datasets", HESS 23:5089, 2019, doi:10.5194/hess-23-5089-2019. One EA-LSTM over 531 basins, mean NSE 0.67-0.71 vs per-basin calibrated HBV ~0.65, mHM ~0.62. -> METHOD for F10: one model with region descriptors as inputs.
3. Tuck, Barratt, Boyd, "A Distributed Method for Fitting Laplacian Regularized Stratified Models", arXiv:1904.12017 (JMLR 2021). Per-stratum models regularized toward neighbouring strata; "produces a model even for categorical values that did not appear in the training data set". -> METHOD for F10 (partial pooling over neuropil adjacency graph).
4. Chen, Deng, Wu, Gu, Li, "Towards Understanding Mixture of Experts in Deep Learning", arXiv:2208.02813, 2022. "The cluster structure of the underlying problem and the non-linearity of the expert are pivotal to the success of MoE." -> CONDITION for F10.
5. Zoph et al., "ST-MoE: Designing Stable and Transferable Sparse Expert Models", arXiv:2202.08906, 2022. "sparse models are prone to overfitting"; CB (250 train) dense beats sparse held-out; ReCoRD (138k) sparse wins "by several percentage points"; CB and WSC (250/259 examples) "the only ones where the sparse model does not yield gains"; expert dropout "failed to improve generalization". -> CONTRADICTS F10 at small per-region n; relates to F5 memorization.
6. Shazeer et al., "Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated MoE Layer", arXiv:1701.06538, ICLR 2017. "model capacity is critical for absorbing the vast quantities of knowledge available in the training corpora"; load-balancing losses needed. -> CONTEXT: MoE is a capacity tool.
7. Jacobs, Jordan, Nowlan, Hinton, "Adaptive mixtures of local experts", Neural Computation 3(1):79-87, 1991. Seminal. NOT text-verified (scanned PDF; metadata from search).
8. Grinsztajn, Oyallon, Varoquaux, "Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?", arXiv:2207.08815 (NeurIPS D&B 2022). "tree-based models remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized data (~10K samples)"; 45 datasets. -> CONFIRMS F5.
9. Chernov, "(GG) MoE vs. MLP on Tabular Data", arXiv:2502.03608, 2025. 38 datasets; GG-MoE best vs MoE/MLP with fewer params. Caveat: no GBDT baseline; single-author preprint. -> EXTENDS F10 weakly.
10. Gorishniy, Kotelnikov, Babenko, "TabM", arXiv:2410.24210, ICLR 2025. Parameter-shared implicit MLP ensemble; "best performance among tabular DL models". -> METHOD.
11. Wang et al., "Graph Mixture of Experts", NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2304.02806. ROC-AUC +1.81% ogbg-molhiv, +1.40% ogbg-molbbbp; experts over hop sizes. -> METHOD for F9/F10 (cf. F7).
12. Zeng et al., "Mixture of Weak & Strong Experts on Graphs" (Mowst), ICLR 2024, arXiv:2311.05185. MLP weak expert + GNN strong expert, confidence-gated. -> METHOD for F9/F10.
13. Silla & Freitas, "A survey of hierarchical classification across different application domains", DMKD 22:31-72, 2011. "an error at a certain class level is going to be propagated downwards the hierarchy"; blocking problem. -> CAUTION for F10 routers.
14. Abdelaal et al., "A comparison of automatic cell identification methods for scRNA-seq data", Genome Biology 2019 (bioRxiv 10.1101/644435). "The general-purpose SVM classifier has overall the best performance"; lower accuracy "for complex datasets with overlapping classes or deep annotations". -> CONFIRMS F1/F5/F6.
15. Michielsen, Reinders, Mahfouz, "Hierarchical progressive learning of cell identities in single-cell data", Nat Commun 12:2799, 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-23196-8. Tree of local SVMs; flat vs hierarchical roughly comparable (fetched summary inconsistent on exact F1 numbers; re-check). -> METHOD for F6.
16. Dominguez Conde et al., Science 376:eabl5197, 2022 (CellTypist; repo fetched, abstract page blocked). One global logistic regression across tissues. -> CONFIRMS.
17. Elabbady et al., "Perisomatic ultrastructure efficiently classifies cells in mouse cortex", Nature 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07765-7. Hierarchy neuron/non-neuron/error -> E/I -> per-class subclass; trained on 1,619-cell column; "cross-validated accuracy of 91% and a dataset-wide test set accuracy of 82%". -> Connectomics precedent for class-conditional experts; shows spatial shift.
18. Eckstein et al., Cell 187:2574, 2024. Single global 3D VGG, neuron-level split; split by neuropil and hemilineage "Accuracy in these split datasets remained high". -> CONFIRMS global model generalizes across regions; context F3.

## Synthesis
- Evidence does not favour region experts by default at 10^3-10^5 neurons. Global models with region as input match or beat per-entity locals (Kratzert, Montero-Manso, CellTypist, Eckstein). Sparse experts overfit small per-expert data (ST-MoE). MoE pays off at huge scale (Shazeer) or with genuine cluster structure (Chen 2022).
- GBDT is already a hard-routed partition ensemble; an anatomical router on top mostly fragments data and worsens the F5 random-vs-grouped gap.
- Top-down routers propagate errors (Silla & Freitas); Elabbady works because level-1 is 96-97% accurate.
- Adopt: (a) baseline = global model + region features that any cluster must beat on grouped and leave-region-out splits; (b) Laplacian-regularized stratified models over neuropil adjacency; (c) route by predicted coarse class (super_class 0.96-0.99) not anatomy; (d) Mowst-style confidence-gated escalation GBDT->GNN; (e) report per-expert n, CI, ECE.
